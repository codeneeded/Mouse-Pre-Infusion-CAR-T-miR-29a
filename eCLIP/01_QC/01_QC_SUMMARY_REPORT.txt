PART A — WHAT WE SAW, AND WHAT IT MEANS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

OVERALL VERDICT: GOOD-QUALITY DATASET. No re-sequencing required, no
library should be dropped. Three caveats shape the analysis (below) but
none invalidate the data.

1) SEQUENCING DEPTH & PROCESSING (IP libraries)
   - Initial reads:      40.99M (IP3) – 56.58M (IP7); mean ~50.6M. Ample.
   - % pass trim:        96.4 – 98.4%. Adapters/quality clean.
   - % uniquely aligned: 46.3 – 64.6%. Healthy for CLIP.
   - % PCR duplicates:   20.4 – 24.2%. LOW -> high library complexity;
                         UMI deduplication performed and effective.
   - Final nonchimeric (AGO2) reads: 10.6M – 19.8M. Deep AGO2 maps.
   - AGO2 peaks (sig.):  5,238 (IP2_Tex_1) – 16,862 (IP3_Teff_2).
   Interpretation: the AGO2 IP and library prep worked across the board.

2) CHIMERIC CAPTURE (the defining miR-eCLIP QC metric)
   - % chimeras:         0.44% – 2.18%; mean ~0.96%. Normal range for
                         miR-eCLIP (chimeras are intentionally a small
                         fraction of reads). Every library captured them.
   - Final chimeric reads: 48,667 (IP2_Tex_1) – 366,713 (IP7_Tn_1).
   Interpretation: chimeric ligation succeeded everywhere; the spread is
   expected and is handled by reproducibility filtering + normalization.

3) DATA vs REPORT RECONCILIATION (we trust the files, not the summary)
   - The per-sample .mir_targets_clusters.bed files carry 23–51% MORE
     cluster rows than the report's "Chimeric clusters" value (consistent,
     not random) — the report applies an extra significance/enrichment
     filter the raw BED does not.
   - Median per-cluster coverage is ~4.7–5.0 reads in EVERY library.
   Interpretation: chimeric clusters are SHALLOW (median ~5 reads), so any
   single cluster is thin evidence. This is precisely why we anchor on the
   REPRODUCIBLE set (present in both replicates) rather than raw per-rep
   clusters, and why the BED rows (analysis input) are ground truth while
   the report numbers are treated as approximate.

4) REPRODUCIBLE YIELD PER STATE (from files)
   - Tn:   1,373 reproducible clusters | 68 distinct miRNAs
   - Teff:   549 reproducible clusters | 81 distinct miRNAs
   - Tmem: 1,152 reproducible clusters | 102 distinct miRNAs
   - Tex:    322 reproducible clusters | 65 distinct miRNAs
   Interpretation: naive shows many clusters but CONCENTRATED in few
   miRNAs (consistent with miR-466i dominance); memory is the most
   DIVERSE (102 miRNAs); exhausted is the thinnest layer.

5) REPLICATE CONCORDANCE (Spearman, per-miRNA target coverage)
   - Teff 0.87 | Tmem 0.86 | Tex 0.79 | Tn 0.71
   Interpretation: all acceptable (>=0.71). Effector/memory are tight;
   naive is the noisiest, reflecting the IP7/IP8 imbalance. (Note: the
   concordance miRNA counts (115–151) exceed the reproducible unique-miRNA
   counts above because concordance uses miRNAs seen in EITHER replicate,
   while the reproducible set requires support in BOTH.)

6) TARGET FEATURE DISTRIBUTION (design-validating)
   - Chimeric targets: Intron 31% | Other 30% | 3'UTR 22% | CDS 17% | 5'UTR 1%
   - AGO2 occupancy:   Intron 35% | 3'UTR 29% | CDS 28% | miRNA 4% | other/5'UTR
   Interpretation: only ~22% of direct miR targets are 3'UTR — restricting
   to 3'UTR would discard ~78% of the data. The large "Other" fraction in
   chimeras (30% vs 2% for AGO2) must be resolved in step 3 annotation
   (ncRNA / antisense / intergenic), NOT discarded.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
DO WE NEED TO CHANGE ANYTHING?
---------------------------------------------------------------------
   * Experiment / sequencing: NO. Depth, complexity, mapping and chimeric
     capture are all sufficient; keep all 8 IP libraries.
   * Analysis safeguards (already in the pipeline plan):
       - Anchor every abundance/target analysis on the REPRODUCIBLE set.
       - Use WITHIN-STATE normalization for cross-state comparison so the
         naive (IP7) depth spike does not dominate.
       - Interpret Tex conservatively: its low target counts are partly a
         capture-efficiency floor, not purely biology. Normalize and word
         "depletion into exhaustion" carefully.
       - Report the naive IP7/IP8 imbalance transparently in the methods.
       - Step 3: use a GENCODE vM25 / mm10 GTF (NOT the CAR-T GRCm39 ref)
         and resolve the chimeric "Other" feature bucket.
   * Optional precision (not required): per-miRNA quantification uses the
     cluster "coverage" column; the chimeric BAM does NOT carry miRNA
     identity, so it adds nothing here.

PART B — MANUSCRIPT METHODS SNIPPET (edit to taste)
=====================================================================

miR-eCLIP and primary data processing.
Direct microRNA–target interactions were profiled by AGO2 miR-eCLIP
(Eclipse Bioinnovations) in CD8+ T cells across four differentiation
states (naive, effector, memory and exhausted), with two biological
replicates per state. For each replicate, an AGO2 immunoprecipitation
(IP) library and a size-matched input library were sequenced. Reads were
processed with the Eclipsebio pipeline: unique molecular identifiers
(UMIs) were used to remove PCR duplicates, adapters were trimmed, and the
genome-mapping (target) portion of chimeric reads was aligned to the
mouse genome (UCSC GRCm38/mm10) with STAR v2.7.7a using end-to-end
alignment. Peaks were annotated against GENCODE release M25. AGO2 read
clusters were called with CLIPper; significant AGO2 peaks were defined as
clusters with >=3 log2 fold enrichment over input and p < 0.001.
microRNA–target chimeric reads were identified, and chimeric ("miRNA
target") clusters were called and assigned to the targeting miRNA.
Reproducible clusters/peaks were defined as those recovered in both
replicates of a condition. Differential AGO2 occupancy and differential
miRNA targeting between states were computed with DESeq2.

Quality control.
Across the eight IP libraries, sequencing depth ranged from 41.0 to 56.6
million reads (mean 50.6 M); 96.4–98.4% of reads passed trimming and
46.3–64.6% aligned uniquely. PCR-duplicate rates were 20.4–24.2%,
indicating high library complexity. Chimeric reads represented 0.44–2.18%
of mapped reads (mean ~0.96%), consistent with expected miR-eCLIP yields,
and all libraries recovered chimeric miRNA–target interactions. Per-miRNA
target coverage was reproducible between replicates (Spearman rho =
0.87, 0.86, 0.79 and 0.71 for effector, memory, exhausted and naive,
respectively). Reproducible chimeric clusters numbered 1,373 (naive,
68 miRNAs), 549 (effector, 81 miRNAs), 1,152 (memory, 102 miRNAs) and
322 (exhausted, 65 miRNAs). Direct miRNA target sites were distributed
across introns (31%), unannotated/other regions (30%), 3' UTRs (22%) and
coding sequence (17%); analyses were therefore not restricted to 3' UTRs.
All downstream analyses used the reproducible cluster sets with
within-state normalization to account for replicate-depth differences,
most notably between the two naive replicates.
